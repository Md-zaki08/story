After Yang is a science-fiction drama set in a near future where families purchase
"technosapiens" — android companions, often purchased as cultural ambassadors to help
adopted children stay connected to their heritage. Jake and Kyra, a couple in a
quietly strained marriage, bought Yang as an older brother for their adopted daughter
Mika. Yang is Chinese-coded: he teaches Mika about tea, heritage, and family rituals,
and becomes the emotional center of the household while Jake, a failed businessman,
drifts at the margins. When Yang suddenly powers down and cannot be restarted, the
family's grief is oddly bureaucratic. Jake takes Yang to a repair shop and then to a
technosapien museum, where he is told Yang's memory core can be partially read.

Jake hires a technician to extract Yang's memories, and the process reveals that Yang
had an inner life the family never suspected: a rich archive of ordinary moments,
private thoughts, and a secret relationship with a young woman named Ada, whom Yang
met through a shared memory-cloning service. The revelation destabilizes Jake. He had
treated Yang as a helpful appliance; Yang had been living a life of his own, with
attachments and regrets. Meanwhile Mika insists that Yang is still her brother and
that the family should not "replace" him. The film's central tension is not whether
Yang is conscious but what obligations follow from having loved something whose inner
life was invisible to you. Jake eventually meets Ada, who copied Yang's memories into
her own home system, and the two share a fragile, grief-stricken intimacy. The film
ends with the family planting a tree for Yang and beginning, haltingly, to talk to one
another again.

Narrative atoms available here include: the service android whose inner life is
discovered only through post-failure memory extraction; the family that purchases a
cultural bridge and then must grieve it; the memory-cloning service that lets strangers
inherit pieces of a dead person; the sibling who refuses the official definition of
death; and the repair quest that becomes a retrospective biography. The film's mode is
melancholy domestic realism rather than action, and its core question is how to mourn
a person you never fully recognized as one.
