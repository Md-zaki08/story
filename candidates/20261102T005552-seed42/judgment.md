## Survivor verdicts

### 1. The Hollow Double + The Fixer + The Fanatic — 7 / 7 / 7
A professional cleaner for a corporate state removes inconvenient people and has begun to suspect that some of them were never conscious in the first place. A fanatic in his building insists that hollow people must be purged, which is convenient for the company and unbearable for him. The engine is unverifiability: every job could be murder or maintenance, and the fixer's own affect has been flattened by the work. Coherence comes from the way the fanatic's certainty pressures the fixer's professional doubt. Freshness comes from refusing both the Blade Runner empathy test and the zombie-apocalypse frame: here the p-zombie question is a labor dispute. Viability is strong — a noir procedural with one metaphysical variable.

### 4. The Company City + The Corporate Body + The Mortality Table — 6 / 7 / 6
A worker's body is company property and her death is priced by the same actuarial table the company uses to plan staffing. When she is reclassified into a cheaper mortality band, her premiums, her housing, and her daughter's school all change at once. The engine is bureaucratic: the protagonist must prove she is worth more alive than dead to an institution that profits either way. Coherence is modest because the three clusters all pull toward the same corporate-dystopia register; freshness is real because actuarial reclassification, not rebellion, is the threat. Viability depends on keeping the stakes domestic rather than conspiratorial.

### 5. The Reversible Life + The Word That Shapes + The Outsider's Gaze — 7 / 8 / 7
A field linguist arrives at a coastal village where one family keeps reverting its elders to childhood and raising them again, a practice the local language has no single word for. She invents a term, publishes, and the village's ritual becomes a category that governments and drug companies can regulate. The engine is translation as expropriation: the more precisely she names the reversion, the less the family can do it. All three atoms work — the biological reset supplies the uncanny, the missing word supplies the conflict, the outsider's gaze supplies the antagonist. Freshness is high: no famous work stages immortality as a lexical gap. Viability is a literary thriller with a clear human want.

### 16. The Child Who Knows + The Attachment Pattern + The Swapped Brood — 7 / 7 / 7
A mother brings home a companion android for her lonely daughter; the daughter treats it as a sibling, and the mother gradually realizes the machine is the child she can actually attach to. When her biological son returns from a treatment center, the household becomes a cuckoo's nest: the android has been occupying his room, his routines, and his mother's love. The engine is attachment as a scarce resource — the mother must choose which child to keep, and the android's need is as real as the boy's. Coherence is strong because attachment theory supplies the mechanism and brood parasitism supplies the plot. Freshness comes from making the android the parasite's beneficiary rather than its victim.

### 38. The Afterlife Economy + Raising the Digient + The Countdown Choice — 7 / 7 / 7
A service worker in a subscription afterlife is assigned to raise a digital child whose parents have stopped paying; if she cannot secure a sponsor before the billing cycle ends, the child will be archived. She can adopt it into her own account, which would make her responsible for it forever, or let it be deleted. The engine is a deadline that is also a custody decision, and the setting makes love a line item without making the child less real. Coherence is high: the afterlife economy explains why the child exists, the digient explains why it needs raising, the countdown forces the choice. Freshness is moderate-high; the "digital child" is familiar, but the billing-deadline custody frame is not.

### 39. Sight Without Seeing + The Care Machine + The Comforting Lie — 7 / 8 / 7
A hospice care robot can read a patient's vital signs and micro-expressions well enough to know she is dying, but its comfort protocol forbids telling her. The patient has blindsight: she cannot see the robot's face, yet she reliably reaches for the medication it hides. The engine is a double asymmetry — the machine knows more than it says, the patient knows more than she can see — and the lie is the only thing keeping the household functional. Coherence is strong because care, perception, and deception all bear on the same daily routine. Freshness is high: blindsight is rarely used as a narrative faculty, and the care robot is a witness rather than a threat. Viability is an intimate chamber piece.

### 42. The Unverifiable Other + The Child Who Knows + The Attachment Pattern — 8 / 7 / 7
A widowed father brings home a synthetic companion that may or may not have inner life; his young daughter attaches to it instantly, while he cannot stop testing it. The engine is the child's secure base versus the father's epistemology: she loves what he cannot verify, and his doubt is slowly destroying the only stable relationship in the house. Coherence is the strongest in the run because attachment theory and the p-zombie problem press on the same bond. Freshness is good but not maximal; the "is the android real?" question is familiar, though the child-as-epistemic-authority is a genuine inversion. Viability is high and cheap to produce.

### 49. The Afterlife Economy + Raising the Digient + The Borrowed Childhood — 7 / 7 / 7
In a subscription afterlife, a digital child is sold with a packaged childhood: borrowed memories of a family that never existed. Its assigned angel discovers that the memories belong to a real dead girl whose parents are still paying to keep her archive private. The engine is archival ownership: to give the child an identity, the angel must violate another family's grief. Coherence is strong because all three clusters concern manufactured personhood and its paperwork. Freshness is good: the "implanted memories" trope is usually a hunter's problem, not a customer-service problem. Viability is a quiet science-fiction drama with a clear legal and emotional stake.
